G - Sources, Notes & Bibliography

A note on sources

This book is intended as an explanation and evaluation of the Marian Calendar rather than as a comprehensive academic history of Martian timekeeping. The bibliography below therefore concentrates on the sources most directly relevant to the architecture discussed in the text.

The history of Martian calendars is considerably larger than the examples examined here. Gangale and Dudley-Rowley's 2005 review identified more than seventy published approaches to Martian timekeeping over the preceding 125 years. The calendars of Levitt, Gangale/Darian, Zubrin, Šurán, Hargitai and Silin are discussed because together they expose many of the principal design choices relevant to Marian: annual scale, weekly recurrence, elapsed-time regularity, seasonal representation, intercalation and familiarity. Gangale and Dudley-Rowley's historical work provides the broader context. ScienceDirect

Definitions and numerical conventions relating to the Martian sol, areocentric solar longitude (Ls), Airy-0 and Martian solar time draw principally upon the work of Michael Allison and Megan McEwen and the NASA Mars24 technical documentation. NASA GIS

The detailed Marian astronomical analysis, intercalation modelling and Earth–Mars communications calculations are documented separately in The Marian Calendar: Design and Evaluation of a Civil Time Architecture for Mars and its associated computational reproducibility package. The book deliberately presents the conclusions of that work rather than reproducing every calculation. The 2026 research preprint is archived at doi:10.5281/zenodo.22687143, and its computational data and reproducibility package at doi:10.5281/zenodo.22686413. These existing deposits are distinct from the separately author-ratified Marian Protocol v1.0, whose authoritative specification and conformance materials are available in the open Marian technical repository.

The discussion of China's Twenty-Four Solar Terms uses them only as a cultural and structural precedent for a named sequence of twenty-four annual positions. They are astronomical and seasonal markers, not civil months and not equivalents of Marian minths. UNESCO inscribed the tradition on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016. UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage
