B - Marian Calendar Tables

Common and Leap Year Structures

B.1 Purpose

This appendix provides the principal structural tables for the Marian Calendar.

It shows the two regular annual templates, the twelve months, twenty-four minths, four quarters, six Sixths and the valid weekday positions at shortened terminal weeks. The tables are derived from the normative rules in Appendix A and are intended as a practical reference for readers, software implementers and future standards work.

Unless otherwise stated, ordinal sol numbers begin with Sol 1 on the first sol of the Marian year.

MY0 is excluded because it is a transitional epoch interval rather than a regular Marian year.

The common-year month pattern is:

The leap-year pattern differs only at the final position:

The additional leap sol is therefore the final sol of Month 12. Nothing earlier in the annual template changes.

B.8 Week structure within a minth

A 28-sol minth consists of four complete seven-position weeks:

7 + 7 + 7 + 7 = 28 sols

A 27-sol minth consists of:

7 + 7 + 7 + 6 = 27 sols

The final week of a 27-sol minth therefore contains weekday positions 1 through 6 only.

Because shortened minths occur only as the second minth of a shortened month, the missing seventh position is simultaneously:

Minth Week 4 · Day 7

and:

Month Week 8 · Day 7

It is one absent civil position described at two structural scales.

B.10 Terminal transitions

The end of every 56-sol month is:

Month n · Week 8 · Day 7

followed by:

Month n+1 · Week 1 · Day 1

The end of every 55-sol month is:

Month n · Week 8 · Day 6

followed immediately by:

Month n+1 · Week 1 · Day 1

The physical sol following Day 6 therefore receives the first weekday label of the new month rather than the unused seventh label of the old one.

For the four common-year quarter boundaries:

Table 11 in Appendix B: Marian Calendar Tables

In a leap year, the final transition differs:

M12 · W8 · D7 → next MY · M1 · W1 · D1

This is why every regular Marian year, month and minth begins at weekday position 1 despite the physical year containing 668 or 669 sols rather than an integer number of ordinary seven-sol weeks.

A regular Marian date is structurally valid if all of the following are true:

Minth week1–4
QuarterQ1–Q4
SixthS1–S6
Ordinal sol1–668 common; 1–669 leap

For Month Week 8 · Day 7, apply one additional test:

  • valid in Months 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10 and 11;
  • invalid in Months 3, 6 and 9;
  • valid in Month 12 only in a leap year.

Equivalently, for a minth date:

  • Week 4 · Day 7 is valid in every 28-sol minth;
  • invalid in every 27-sol minth.

No other weekday position changes validity between the two regular annual templates.

B.16 The whole architecture in one line

For reference, the common Marian year can be reconstructed from a remarkably small set of relationships:

A common year has 668 sols, arranged as 12 months (56–56–55 ×4), 24 minths, 6 Sixths and 4 quarters.

and the leap year by one additional instruction:

restore the final Day 7 of Month 12 → 669 sols

Everything else in these tables follows from those two statements.
